New York State

CPLR Article 78. Challenge a New York state or local agency, officer, or court.

What happened?

  • The law says an official must do a certain thing. The official has no choice about it and has not done it. You ask the court to order it done.

    Mandamus to Compel · §7803(1) · Klostermann v. Cuomo, 61 N.Y.2d 525, 539 (1984)

  • A court or officer is acting without the power to act. You ask the court to make it stop.

    Prohibition · §7803(2) · La Rocca v. Lane, 37 N.Y.2d 575, 579 (1975)

  • An agency made a choice it was allowed to make, but made it the wrong way. It skipped a required step, got the law wrong, or had no sound reason. You ask the court to undo it.

    Mandamus to Review · §7803(3) · Pell v. Board of Education, 34 N.Y.2d 222, 231 (1974)

  • An agency held a hearing with sworn witnesses and then ruled. You ask the court to check that the proof from the hearing backs up the ruling.

    Substantial Evidence · §7803(4) · 300 Gramatan Ave. Assoc. v. State Div. of Human Rights, 45 N.Y.2d 176, 180 (1978)

How long do you have?

Four months from the final decision (CPLR §217). To compel an official to act, four months from the refusal of your written demand.

Can you file yet?

Article 78 only works on a final decision. If the law lets you ask the agency to hear the matter again, you must ask first, or wait until the time to ask has run out. You cannot use Article 78 to attack a ruling made in a civil or criminal case. The one exception is when a court punished someone on the spot for contempt (CPLR §7801).

Steps.

  1. Record

    Put every demand, refusal, and objection in writing, with the date and proof of receipt.

  2. Filing

    File a notice of petition and a verified petition in Supreme Court, in the county CPLR §506(b) names. If time is short, use an order to show cause.

  3. Service

    Serve the other side at least twenty days before the court date (§7804(c)). For a state body or officer, also serve the Attorney General, at the office in or nearest that county. For a member of a local body whose term has ended, serve the body (CPLR §311).

  4. Answer

    The other side files a sworn answer and the certified record (§7804(e)).

  5. Transfer

    Substantial-evidence cases (§7803(4)) move to the Appellate Division, after the trial court rules on jurisdiction, the time limit, and res judicata (§7804(g)).

Can the decision be paused?

The court can put the decision on hold while the case goes on. It can set terms, such as paying money as security. If the Appellate Division made the ruling, only that court or the Court of Appeals can put it on hold (CPLR §7805).

What can the court do?

At the end, the court can give you what you asked for or throw the case out. It can cancel the decision, keep it, or change it. It can order the other side to act or to stop. You can get money only when it is tied to the main thing you asked for (CPLR §7806).

Glossary.

Appellate Division
The appeals court above Supreme Court. It sits in four departments.
Contempt
Disobeying or disrespecting a court.
Court of Appeals
The highest court in New York State.
CPLR
Civil Practice Law and Rules, the New York statute governing civil procedure.
Jurisdiction
The legal power of a court or officer to act in a matter.
Mandamus
A court order that tells an official to carry out a duty.
Notice of petition
The paper that tells the other side about the case and the court date.
Order to show cause
A paper a judge signs that sets a fast court date. It takes the place of a notice of petition when time is short.
Prohibition
A court order that tells a court or officer to stop acting without power.
Record
The papers and testimony the agency or court had in front of it when it decided.
Res judicata
The rule that a matter already decided cannot be brought again.
Service
Giving court papers to the other side in the way the law requires.
Substantial evidence
Enough proof that a reasonable person could accept it as support for the decision.
Supreme Court
The main trial court. It is not the highest court.
Verified petition
The paper that starts the case. It says what happened and what you want. Verified means you swear it is true.
