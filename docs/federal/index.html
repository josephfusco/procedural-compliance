What happened?

  • A federal official or agency must do a certain thing for you and has no choice about it. You have tried every other way to get it done. You ask a federal trial court to order it.

    This works only against the federal government. It does not work against a state court, a state clerk, or a state agency. For those, go to state court. In New York State, that is Article 78.

    1. File a complaint in a federal trial court (28 U.S.C. §1361; Fed. R. Civ. P. 3).
    2. File it where the official or agency is, or where the events happened. If no land is involved, you can also file where you live (28 U.S.C. §1391(e)(1)).
    3. Have someone who is at least 18 and not part of the case serve the summons and complaint (Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(c)(2)).
    4. Serve the United States attorney for that district. Send copies by registered or certified mail to the Attorney General in Washington and to the official or agency (Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(i)).
    5. The government has 60 days to answer after the United States attorney is served (Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(a)(2)).

    Mandamus to Compel a Federal Officer · 28 U.S.C. §1361 · Heckler v. Ringer, 466 U.S. 602, 616 (1984)

  • A federal trial court is doing something it has no power to do, and a later appeal cannot fix the harm. You ask the appeals court above it to step in.

    File your petition with the clerk of the appeals court. Give a copy to every party and to the trial judge. The appeals court can say no without asking anyone to answer (Fed. R. App. P. 21(a)(1), (b)(1)).

    Mandamus to a Lower Court · 28 U.S.C. §1651(a) · Fed. R. App. P. 21 · Cheney v. United States District Court, 542 U.S. 367, 380–81 (2004)

What if you cannot pay the fee?

Ask the court to let you file without paying the fee first. You file a sworn statement of what you own and that you cannot pay (28 U.S.C. §1915(a)(1)). If the court agrees, it must also order a United States marshal to serve your papers (Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(c)(3)).

Glossary.

Agency
A part of the government that carries out the law.
Complaint
The paper that starts the case. It says what happened and what you want.
Fed. R. App. P.
Federal Rules of Appellate Procedure.
Fed. R. Civ. P.
Federal Rules of Civil Procedure.
Mandamus
A court order that tells an official or a lower court to carry out a duty.
Petition
The paper that asks the court for the order.
Serve
Give court papers to the other side in the way the law requires.
Summons
The paper that tells the other side it is being sued and must answer.
