Federal

Mandamus. Compel a federal official, agency, or lower court to do its duty.

What happened?

  • A federal official or agency must do a certain thing for you and has no choice about it. You have tried every other way to get it done. You ask a federal trial court to order it.

    This works only against the federal government. It does not work against a state court, a state clerk, or a state agency. For those, go to state court. In New York State, that is Article 78.

    Mandamus to Compel a Federal Officer · 28 U.S.C. §1361 · Heckler v. Ringer, 466 U.S. 602, 616 (1984)

  • A federal trial court is doing something it has no power to do, and a later appeal cannot fix the harm. You ask the appeals court above it to step in.

    File your petition with the clerk of the appeals court. Give a copy to every party and to the trial judge. The appeals court can say no without asking anyone to answer (Fed. R. App. P. 21(a)(1), (b)(1)).

    Mandamus to a Lower Court · 28 U.S.C. §1651(a) · Fed. R. App. P. 21 · Cheney v. United States District Court, 542 U.S. 367, 380–81 (2004)
