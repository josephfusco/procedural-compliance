---
order: 3
statute: "CPLR §506(b)(1)"
name: "Original jurisdiction misrouted"
rule: "An Article 78 against a Supreme Court justice or County Court judge is original jurisdiction in the Appellate Division — not Supreme Court — in the judicial department where the action is triable."
breach: "Trial-court intake redirects such petitions back to Supreme Court — the very court whose justices §506(b)(1) precludes from passing on each other."
authority:
  - source: "CPLR §506(b)(1)"
  - source: "CPLR §7804(b)"
  - case: "Nolan v. Lungen"
    cite: "61 N.Y.2d 788 (1984)"
why: "Misrouting restarts the four-month clock from a filing date the petitioner did not choose, with no record-creation event later citable."
happened: "Your case against a judge was sent to the wrong court."
plain: "A case against a Supreme Court or County Court judge must start in the Appellate Division. Clerks sometimes send it back to Supreme Court, which is the wrong place. That moves your filing date, and the four-month limit is counted from it."
