order: 6
statute: "CPLR §7803(1) · §7803(3)"
name: "Pell deference imported into mandamus"
rule: "Mandamus to compel under §7803(1) lies as of right where there is a clear ministerial duty. The arbitrary-and-capricious standard of Pell governs only §7803(3) review of agency discretion."
breach: "Courts apply Pell deference to mandamus-to-compel petitions — finding the official's refusal rational and denying relief — even where the duty is non-discretionary."
authority:
  - case: "Klostermann v. Cuomo"
    cite: "61 N.Y.2d 525 (1984)"
  - case: "Brusco v. Braun"
    cite: "84 N.Y.2d 674 (1994)"
  - case: "Matter of Council of City of N.Y. v. Bloomberg"
    cite: "6 N.Y.3d 380 (2006)"
  - case: "Matter of Pell v. Bd. of Educ."
    cite: "34 N.Y.2d 222 (1974)"
why: "Appellate panels conflate the two §7803 subdivisions. Pell-shorthand affirmance is functionally unreviewable — the standard-confusion lives at the panel level."
happened: "The court asked whether the refusal made sense."
plain: "When an official has no choice but to act, the court should order the act. It should not ask whether the refusal made sense. Courts sometimes use that softer test anyway and deny the order."
